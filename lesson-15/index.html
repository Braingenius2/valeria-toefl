⚡ Evidence Drill: Verify Every Word

Instruction: Choose the statement that is not supported by each passage. Commit to an answer before revealing the explanation.

Item 1

Passage: "While early solar panels operated at less than ten percent efficiency, modern industrial photovoltaics frequently exceed twenty-two percent."

(A) Solar technology efficiency has more than doubled over time.
(B) Early solar panels were completely incapable of generating usable electricity.
(C) Current industrial panels can convert over one-fifth of absorbed sunlight into energy.
Answer: B. The passage gives an efficiency comparison; it does not support the absolute claim that early panels were completely incapable of producing usable electricity.
Item 2

Passage: "After the university introduced free evening buses, ridership rose by 30 percent during the first month. A student survey found that many commuters appreciated the service, while some students still preferred walking or cycling."

(A) Some students appreciated the evening bus service.
(B) Every student preferred buses to walking or cycling.
(C) Ridership increased during the first month.
Answer: B. The passage says that some students still preferred walking or cycling, so the word every overstates the evidence.
✍️ 10-Minute Academic Discussion: Remote Work vs. Office
10:00
Task: State and support your opinion. Make a meaningful contribution using the classmates' ideas. Minimum: 100 words. Practice target: 120-130 words.

Professor: "Some companies are mandating a full return to the office, arguing that in-person collaboration drives innovation and company culture. Others advocate for permanent remote work, citing higher employee satisfaction and reduced commuting stress. What is your view?"

Rachel: "In-person interaction is essential. Spontaneous conversations lead to creative breakthroughs that Zoom cannot replicate."
David: "Remote work saves employees hundreds of hours in commuting and lowers overhead costs. Hybrid models are much better."
Word count: 0 words (Minimum: 100; target: 120-130)
Strong sample (task score 4-5 range):

I agree with David that remote work benefits employees and employers because it removes long commutes and lowers office costs. These savings can improve workers' well-being and help companies invest in digital collaboration. Rachel is right that spontaneous office conversations can produce useful ideas; however, remote teams can create similar opportunities through regular video meetings and informal online channels. For example, a team could hold a weekly brainstorming session where everyone shares an early idea before developing it further. This arrangement could also make recruitment more inclusive for people who live far from major cities. In my view, companies should not require a full return to the office. A flexible remote or hybrid policy is more effective because it preserves collaboration while giving employees greater control over their time.

✉️ Write an Email: 5-Part Framework
1. Greeting and purpose — Dear Professor [Name]. Thank you for asking...
2. Content point 1 — state your opinion and explain why.
3. Content point 2 — make the requested suggestion and add a detail.
4. Content point 3 — offer help or state the requested next step.
5. Polite close — Please let me know... + Best regards, [Name]
Key Phrases Bank: "Thank you for asking students to..." · "I support this proposal because..." · "One useful improvement would be..." · "I would be happy to help..." · "Please let me know if..."

7-Minute Plan: 1 min — read the 3 content points · 4 min — write · 2 min — check (practice target: 100-120 words, clear paragraphs, appropriate tone)
✉️ Write an Email (7 min, 100-120 words)
07:00
Email from Professor Chen: "The university is considering keeping the main library open until midnight on weekdays. I would like your opinion on this proposal."

In your email:
  1. Say whether you support the extended hours and explain why.
  2. Suggest one improvement that would make the new hours more useful.
  3. Offer to help collect student opinions about the proposal.
Word count: 0 words (Minimum: 100; target: 100-120)
Strong sample (task score 4-5 range):

Dear Professor Chen,

Thank you for asking students to comment on the proposed library hours. I support keeping the main library open until midnight because many students work part-time during the day and need a quiet place to study in the evening.

One useful improvement would be to create a separate silent zone. It would also be helpful to provide a small area for group work so that conversations do not disturb other students.

I would be happy to help collect student opinions about the new schedule. Please let me know if you would like me to share a short survey with my classmates. This would give the university useful feedback before making a permanent decision.

Best regards,
Valeria

📚 Homework: Two Write an Email Tasks

Complete both tasks this week. Each response has a 7-minute practice timer and a 100-120 word target. The framework is flexible, not a script: adapt it to the recipient, tone, and every requested content point.

Adaptable email skeleton
  1. Greeting and purpose: address the recipient and explain why you are writing.
  2. Direct answer: give your position or answer to the main request with a reason.
  3. Requested detail: answer the next point with one specific detail or suggestion.
  4. Action or next step: offer help, ask a question, or state what should happen next.
  5. Close: finish politely and sign your name.

Do not force a block that the prompt does not need. Map every bullet point to a clear sentence or paragraph.

✉️ Homework 1: Guided Email
07:00
Scenario (ETS Practice Test 2): Your coworker, Kevin, recently recommended a new restaurant for your team to visit. You took the team there, but everyone was disappointed. The food was not as good as expected, and the service was slow. You need to inform Kevin about the situation and discuss future lunch options.

In your email:
  1. Explain what was wrong with the restaurant.
  2. Describe the team's reaction to the visit.
  3. Suggest alternative lunch arrangements.
Sentence starters you may adapt (register: semi-formal — coworker)

Hi Kevin, I am writing to let you know... / Unfortunately, the food was not as good as we expected... / It might be better if... / Would it be possible to...? / Please let me know what you think. / Best, [Name]

Word count: 0 words (Minimum: 100; target: 100-120)
Model answer (~115 words, semi-formal):

Hi Kevin,

Thank you for recommending that restaurant for our team lunch. Unfortunately, the food was not as good as we expected, and the service was quite slow, so everyone was disappointed.

To be specific, the main dishes arrived cold, and we waited almost thirty minutes for the bill. Most of the team said they would prefer a place with faster service next time.

It might be better to try a casual café near the office instead. There is a new bistro on Market Street with good reviews, and we could book a table for Wednesday. Would it be possible for you to join us there?

Please let me know what you think.

Best,
[Name]

✉️ Homework 2: Independent Email
07:00
Scenario (ETS Teacher Resources Practice Test 2): You have been working on a group project with several classmates for a course. One of your group members, Alex, has not been participating actively and has missed several assignments. You want to address this issue and find a solution.

In your email (register: polite but direct — classmate in an academic project):
  1. Describe the current progress of the group project.
  2. Explain how his lack of participation is affecting the group.
  3. Suggest ways he can contribute to the project moving forward.
Final check before submitting: answer all three points, use an appropriate tone, include specific details, and check your word count and obvious errors.
Word count: 0 words (Minimum: 100; target: 100-120)
Model answer (~120 words, polite but direct):

Hi Alex,

I hope you are doing well. I wanted to check in about our group project, since we are almost halfway through the deadline.

So far, we have finished the introduction and the first two sections of the report. However, your section is still missing, and the group has had to cover your part of the presentation preparation as well. This has made it harder for us to stay on schedule.

It would be great if you could send us your draft by Thursday. Even a rough version would help, and we could divide the remaining tasks so everyone contributes equally.

Please let me know if you need any help getting started. We would really like to finish this together.

Best,
[Name]
