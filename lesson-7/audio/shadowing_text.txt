Algorithms now govern many aspects of our daily lives, from the news we see to the loans we qualify for. Proponents argue that algorithmic decision-making is more efficient and less biased than human judgment. Critics, however, contend that algorithms can perpetuate and even amplify existing inequalities, creating feedback loops that reinforce discrimination. The challenge lies in designing systems that are both effective and equitable, a goal that requires diverse perspectives in development and oversight. The future of fair governance depends on our ability to hold algorithmic systems accountable.
