📐 The 5-Sentence Progression

Forget the old "3 templates" approach. Every Academic Discussion response follows the same logical progression — not a rigid template, but a sequence of 5 rhetorical moves. Master this and you never get stuck after sentence 2.

# Function What to do Sample opener
1 Position State your stance — agree, disagree, or nuanced "I strongly agree with [name]... / While I understand [name]'s perspective..."
2 Acknowledge Reference a classmate's point specifically "Aisha raises a compelling point about..."
3 Counter Push the discussion forward with your own argument "However, I would add that... / Nevertheless, this overlooks..."
4 Evidence Give a concrete example or reason "For example, the COMPAS algorithm shows that..."
5 Tie-back Connect back to the professor's question "In conclusion, the question is not whether... but whether..."

💬 Academic Discussion Thread
👨‍🏫 Professor Chen:

"Let's discuss the role of algorithms in criminal justice. Some argue that AI risk assessment tools can reduce bias in sentencing. Others warn that these systems may reinforce historical patterns of discrimination. What's your perspective?"

👩 Aisha (classmate):

"I think AI tools are a step forward. Human judges are inconsistent — some are lenient, others harsh. Algorithms apply the same criteria to everyone. If we train them on clean data, they can help eliminate the racial disparities we see in the current system."

👨 Daniel (classmate):

"I'm skeptical. Algorithms are trained on historical data, which reflects past discrimination. If you feed biased data into an AI, you get biased decisions out. We saw this with COMPAS, the risk assessment tool that falsely flagged Black defendants as high-risk at much higher rates."

✍️ Timed Academic Discussion — AI in Hiring
👨‍🏫 Professor's Prompt

"Some companies now use AI to screen job applications before a human ever sees them. Supporters claim this speeds up hiring and reduces human bias. Critics argue that AI screening can discriminate against certain groups and lacks transparency. Should AI have a role in hiring? What's your view?"

👩 Priya (classmate):

"AI screening is more objective than human recruiters. A machine doesn't care about your name, gender, or appearance — it only evaluates skills and experience. Companies that don't use it are at a competitive disadvantage."

👨 David (classmate):

"Studies have shown that AI screening tools can discriminate against women and minorities because they're trained on historical hiring data that reflects past bias. Amazon scrapped its AI hiring tool because it penalized resumes containing the word 'women's.'"

Position Acknowledge Counter Evidence Tie-back

1. Position: While I understand Priya's argument that AI screening removes human bias, I believe the risks of perpetuating hiring discrimination outweigh the efficiency benefits.

2. Acknowledge: Priya makes a fair point — algorithms do not care about a candidate's name, gender, or appearance in the way humans might.

3. Counter: Nevertheless, as David rightly notes, AI systems are trained on historical data that reflects existing biases, so they risk automating rather than eliminating discrimination.

4. Evidence: The Amazon hiring algorithm is a clear example — it was scrapped after penalizing resumes containing the word "women's," having learned from a male-dominated tech workforce.

5. Tie-back: Ultimately, the question is not whether AI can be faster than human recruiters, but whether we can build fair enough training data for it to be truly equitable.
