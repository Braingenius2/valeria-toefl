🎙️ Shadowing — Argument Structure
Strong academic arguments follow a four-part framework that demonstrates both analytical thinking and persuasive skill. The claim states the writer's position clearly. The evidence supports the claim with specific examples, data, or reasoning. The counterargument acknowledges opposing viewpoints, showing intellectual honesty. The rebuttal refutes the counterargument, reinforcing the original claim. This structure — claim, evidence, counterargument, rebuttal — transforms a simple opinion into a sophisticated argument that meets the expectations of formal academic discourse and scores highly on the TOEFL writing assessment.

Targets: four-part framework · analytical thinking · persuasive skill · counterargument · rebuttal · intellectual honesty

🏗️ The C-E-C-R Framework

A strong argument has 4 parts. In a short response, you may not have space for all 4 — but aim for at least 3 (C-E-R or C-E-C).

C — Claim

States your position. 1 sentence. Clear stance.

"Universities should prioritise funding for student mental health services."

E — Evidence

Supports the claim with examples, data, or reasoning. 2–3 sentences.

"Research demonstrates that students who access counselling services show higher retention rates. Furthermore, mental health issues are a leading cause of dropout in higher education."

C — Counterargument

Acknowledges the opposing view. 1 sentence. Shows intellectual honesty.

"Critics argue that funding should prioritise academic resources rather than support services."

R — Rebuttal

Refutes the counterargument. 1 sentence. Reinforces the claim.

"However, without adequate mental health support, academic resources cannot be used effectively."

TOEFL tip: The counterargument + rebuttal pair is what separates 4.0+ from 3.0 responses. It shows you can consider multiple perspectives.

Concession language — counterargument → rebuttal

Although / While + clause: Although critics argue that regulation slows growth, ...
Despite / In spite of + noun: Despite these concerns, ...
Even if / Even though + clause: Even if the costs are high, ...
Critics argue that… / It is true that… ; However, ...

⚠️ Never “Although… but…” — one connector per sentence.
