🧭 The Reading Section — What Changed

~18–27 minutes · 35–48 items · adaptive: the second set adjusts to your first-set performance — a strong start matters. Three task types:

Complete the Words

Fill the missing letters. Context + spelling. Fast — don't stall on one word.

Read in Daily Life

Notices, emails, campus messages + questions. Detail-focused: find the exact line.

Read an Academic Passage

Passage + questions: main idea, detail, inference, vocabulary-in-context, negative fact.

⚡ Three rules for reading under time

1 · Questions first. Read the questions, underline keywords, then read the text — you read looking for answers, not for the sake of reading.
2 · Never leave blanks. There is no penalty for wrong answers — a guess is a chance, an empty box is a zero.
3 · 90-second rule. Any single item that takes longer than ~90 seconds: guess, flag it, move on. One stuck item costs two later items.

📖 Read an Academic Passage (12 min)
12:00

Urban Trees

Cities are increasingly investing in trees — not as decoration, but as infrastructure. A mature street tree can lower surrounding air temperature by several degrees on hot days, because leaves release water vapour and shade surfaces that would otherwise absorb heat. This cooling effect reduces the demand for air conditioning in nearby buildings, cutting both energy costs and carbon emissions.

Trees also intercept rainwater before it reaches drains. A single mature tree can absorb hundreds of litres of storm water each year, which reduces flooding during heavy rain and eases pressure on ageing drainage systems. For city planners, this makes trees a form of 'green infrastructure' — cheaper than concrete pipes and with additional benefits.

Those benefits include public health. Studies in several countries link neighbourhoods with more trees to lower stress levels and better mental health outcomes, though researchers caution that such correlations do not prove that trees cause the improvements. What is clearer is the effect on air quality: leaves trap particulate pollution, removing it from the air that residents breathe.

The challenge is maintenance. Trees need water, pruning and protection during their first years, and budgets for care are often cut before budgets for planting. As a result, many cities lose a significant share of new trees within a decade — a reminder that planting is the beginning of the work, not the end.

1. What is the main idea of the passage?
A Cities should plant more trees for decoration.   B Urban trees function as valuable city infrastructure.   C Drainage systems are cheaper than trees.   D Air conditioning causes most urban emissions.
2. How do trees reduce air conditioning demand?
A By blocking sunlight only.   B By releasing water vapour and providing shade.   C By trapping pollution particles.   D By absorbing storm water.
3. Why does the author mention "concrete pipes"?
A To show trees can replace some built infrastructure at lower cost.   B To criticise modern drainage systems.   C To explain how pipes are installed.   D To argue that trees are more expensive.
4. What do researchers caution about the health studies?
A The samples were too small.   B The studies covered only one country.   C Correlation does not prove causation.   D Mental health is difficult to measure.
5. The word "intercept" in paragraph 2 is closest in meaning to:
A catch   B clean   C direct   D predict
1 B — the whole passage argues trees = infrastructure.  2 B — "leaves release water vapour and shade surfaces".  3 A — "cheaper than concrete pipes and with additional benefits".  4 C — "correlations do not prove that trees cause the improvements".  5 A — intercept = catch (rainwater before it reaches drains).

🎤 Take an Interview — 4 Questions, No Prep

Exam rules: zero preparation time · one take · 30–45 seconds per answer · natural and complete, not rushed. The examiner starts with the first question and follows the conversation organically.

Q1

What is one skill you would like to learn this year, and why?

Q2

Do you prefer studying alone or in a group? Why?

Q3

Describe a time when you had to manage several tasks at once.

Q4

Some students choose to study abroad. Would you? Why or why not?

Model (~60 words — one skill, one reason, one concrete detail):

One skill I would like to learn this year is public speaking, because I want to present my ideas more confidently at university. I have started by practising short talks in front of a mirror, and I record myself once a week to check my pace and clarity. It feels uncomfortable, but that awkwardness is exactly why the skill is worth building.

Notice the shape: answer → reason → habit/detail → one closing line. One idea, fully developed — not three half-ideas.
Answered within 30–45 seconds?
One clear reason + one concrete detail?
No long initial pause (start within ~2 seconds)?
Natural tone — not an essay voice?
No mid-answer restarts (one take)?
Homework: record all four, one take each. Best one + one sentence why it was best.
