Reading Passage — Cognitive Dissonance
Cognitive Dissonance

Cognitive dissonance is a psychological theory proposed by Leon Festinger in 1957. The theory states that individuals experience mental discomfort when they hold two or more contradictory beliefs, values, or attitudes simultaneously. This discomfort motivates people to reduce the inconsistency by changing one of the conflicting elements — typically by altering their attitude rather than their behavior. According to Festinger, the greater the discrepancy between a person's actions and beliefs, the stronger the pressure to resolve the dissonance. People may rationalize their behavior, seek out confirming information, or avoid situations that highlight the contradiction.

Lecture & Synthesis Grid

How does the professor illustrate cognitive dissonance?

Reading (Concept)

  • Mental discomfort from contradictory beliefs.
  • Reduce inconsistency by changing attitude, not behavior.
  • Rationalize behavior to resolve discrepancy.

Lecture (Examples)

  • Ex 1: Smokers. Know health risks, but rationalize (e.g., "I'll quit later", "My grandpa smoked"). Adjust belief, not behavior.
  • Ex 2: Effort Justification. Participants with severe initiation to a boring group inflated its value to justify their suffering.
C1 Upgrades:
Instead of "change their mind" → rationalize / justify their behavior
Instead of "feel bad" → experience psychological discomfort
Instead of "fix the problem" → reconcile the inconsistency
Controlled Practice — Synthesis Drills

Tap to reveal answers. Use academic register vocabulary.

1. The reading passage defines cognitive dissonance as the mental discomfort that arises when an individual holds __________.
Answer: two or more contradictory beliefs, values, or attitudes simultaneously
2. According to Festinger's theory, people reduce dissonance primarily by changing their __________ rather than their __________.
Answer: attitude(s) … behavior
3. The professor's first example involves smokers who __________ their habit by telling themselves that the health risks do not apply to them personally.
Answer: rationalize (also acceptable: justify)
4. In the second example, participants who underwent a __________ initiation rated a boring group as more valuable, demonstrating the effort justification effect.
Answer: severe / difficult
5. The participants in the initiation experiment __________ their evaluation of the group to justify the discomfort they had endured.
Answer: inflated
6. Complete the synthesis frame: "The reading explains that cognitive dissonance is __________. The professor illustrates this with two examples. First, __________. Second, __________."
Answer: "...the psychological discomfort caused by holding contradictory beliefs. The professor illustrates this with two examples. First, smokers rationalize their habit despite knowing the health risks by telling themselves exceptions apply. Second, participants who endured a difficult initiation inflated their evaluation of a boring group to justify the effort they had invested."
7. Rewrite using academic vocabulary: "Smokers feel bad because they know smoking is bad but they keep doing it, so they tell themselves it's OK."
Answer: "Smokers experience psychological discomfort because their behavior contradicts their knowledge of the health risks. To reconcile this inconsistency, they engage in self-justification, rationalizing that the risks do not personally apply to them."
