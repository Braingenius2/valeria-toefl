🎙️ Shadowing: Strategy Language
Academic discourse demands precise collocational competence to achieve lexical sophistication. When students deploy phrases like 'conduct research' or 'fulfill requirements' with natural fluency, they simultaneously demonstrate both analytical depth and native-like command. Mastering these fixed expressions is essential for reaching advanced proficiency thresholds.

Targets: collocational · deploy · fluency · demonstrate · command · fixed expressions · proficiency

📚 10 Essential C1 Collocations
conduct research✅ "The lab conducts research on solar energy."❌ do a research / make research
fulfill requirements✅ "She fulfilled all the graduation requirements."❌ execute requirements (калька «выполнять»)
acquire knowledge✅ "Students acquire knowledge through practice."❌ learn knowledge
draw conclusions✅ "We drew conclusions from the survey data."❌ make conclusions
gain experience✅ "He gained experience at a software company."❌ make experience
demonstrate competence✅ "She demonstrated competence in data analysis."⚠️ competence in a field, not "on"
establish credibility✅ "The study established credibility for the method."❌ build a credibility (no article)
address issues⚠️ register upgrade: solve problems ✔ neutral → address issues ✔ academic (both are correct)
achieve success✅ achieve success · achieve a goal⚠️ succeed in doing ≠ achieve a goal
provide evidence✅ "The results provide evidence for the theory."❌ give an evidence (uncountable)
⚠️ Transfer Traps & Register Upgrades
Trap 1: "do a research" ← Russian has no articles and uses делать → conduct research (research is uncountable)
Trap 2: "make conclusions" ← делать выводы → draw conclusions
Trap 3: "learn knowledge" ← учить знания → acquire / gain knowledge
Trap 4: article insertion: "a research", "an evidence" ← Russian has no articles → research, evidence (uncountable)
Upgrade 1: solve problems / issues ✔ correct but neutral → address issues in academic writing
Upgrade 2: succeed ≠ achieve: you succeed in doing something, but you achieve success / achieve a goal
📖 Reading: Vocabulary in Context

Instruction: Choose the closest meaning. Commit to an answer before revealing.

Item 1

"Early results suggest that regular practice helps students acquire knowledge more efficiently, but researchers must conduct further studies before they can draw firm conclusions about long-term benefits."

(A) physically sketch a diagram
(B) form an opinion after reasoning from evidence
(C) memorize the results exactly
Answer: B. Draw conclusions means to form an opinion based on evidence — the same meaning as in "we drew conclusions from the survey data".
Item 2

"Although the new tutoring program addressed many student issues, its organizers provided little evidence that grades had improved, so the school board hesitated to fund it."

(A) created new problems for students
(B) began to deal with those problems
(C) wrote their addresses down
Answer: B. Address issues means to start dealing with them. Note that evidence stays singular here because it is uncountable — no "an" or "-s".

✏️ Error Correction Drill

Fix each sentence. Some are grammar traps; one is already correct — decide which.

1. "She do a research on climate change."
2. "He get knowledge from online courses."
3. "They make conclusions from the data."
4. "The teacher have a lot of experience."
5. "We need to solve these issues."
6. "The report gives an evidence that the method works."
"She do a research on climate change." → "She conducts research on climate change." (verb agreement + uncountable)
"He get knowledge from online courses." → "He acquires knowledge from online courses." (agreement + academic verb)
"They make conclusions from the data." → "They draw conclusions from the data."
"The teacher have a lot of experience." → "The teacher has a lot of experience." (agreement)
"We need to solve these issues." ✔ already correct — academic register upgrade: "We need to address these issues."
"...gives an evidence..." → "...provides evidence that the method works." (uncountable, no article)
