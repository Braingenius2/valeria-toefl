The rise of the gig economy has fundamentally transformed the nature of work in the twenty-first century. Platforms like Uber, Upwork, and Fiverr have given workers unprecedented flexibility, allowing them to choose when, where, and how much they work. Yet this flexibility comes at a cost, the absence of traditional employment protections such as health insurance, paid leave, and retirement benefits. As more workers move from permanent positions to freelance arrangements, policymakers face the urgent task of creating a social safety net that protects all workers, regardless of employment classification.
