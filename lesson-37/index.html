Scenario information
Housing Office message: You have been assigned Hall B, Room 214, beside the main entrance for the spring term. The corridor is often noisy late at night. Room-change requests are due Friday; quieter rooms in Hall C may be available, depending on capacity. Your situation: you have an 8 a.m. laboratory class three mornings a week.

Write a reply to the Housing Office. In your email, do the following: 1. Identify your current room assignment. 2. Explain why you are requesting a change and which alternative you would prefer. 3. Ask which form or steps are required, whether a fee applies, and when a decision may be made. Practice target: about 100-120 words. This is an internal coaching band, not an ETS-stated word-count requirement.

Dear Housing Office,

I am writing about my spring assignment to Hall B, Room 214, next to the main entrance. The corridor is often noisy late at night. Since I attend an 8 a.m. laboratory class three mornings a week, the noise is affecting my sleep and concentration. A quieter room would help me arrive rested and participate fully in my practical work.

Could I be considered for a quieter room in Hall C if one is available? I understand that room changes depend on availability. Please let me know which form I need to complete, whether a fee applies, and when I might receive a decision.

Thank you for your help.

Best regards,
Valeria

Post-test · review

Review & repair

Before we finish

  • Bullet coverage: all three answered; bullet 2 planned first and developed with relevant detail.
  • Length: 100-120 words is our internal practice band; it is not an ETS word-count requirement.
  • Writing rate: words divided by active time on the 7-minute timer, not time since the first keystroke.
  • After the timed response is submitted, use 90 seconds for a separate diagnostic proofread; do not revise the scored first draft.
  • Proofread targets: known spelling list, sentence boundaries, and a complete final sentence.
