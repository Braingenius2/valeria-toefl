🎙️ Shadowing: Writing Under Time Pressure
Efficient academic writing under timed conditions requires writers to manage both speed and accuracy simultaneously. Successful test-takers develop pre-planned structural frames, allocate roughly one minute per sentence, and reserve the final minute for proofreading critical errors. Rather than pursuing perfect opening sentences, experienced writers begin with their strongest argument and build outward. This strategic approach allows candidates to produce substantive responses that meet length requirements while maintaining grammatical precision and lexical variety.

Targets: manage speed and accuracy · pre-planned structural frames · one minute per sentence · proofreading pass · substantive responses · grammatical precision · lexical variety

🔧 Error Surgery: Top 3 Writing Fixes

Three focused drills targeting the #1 cap on both writing tasks: speed + length.

Fix 1: Generation Speed — Chunk-Writing (10 min)

Write in chunks of 25–30 words (one idea = one chunk). Do NOT stop to edit mid-sentence. Set a 4-minute timer and write 60+ words on this prompt:

Academic Discussion (TOEFL format): Your professor is teaching a course on university policy. Write a post responding to her question. In your post, you should: express and support your opinion; make a contribution to the discussion in your own words. Drill target: 60+ words in 4 minutes.

Professor: "This week we are discussing graduation requirements. Should universities require all students to complete a community service project before graduation? Why or why not?"

Nina: "Service work builds practical skills that ordinary classes cannot teach, and it strengthens the community at the same time."
Tom: "Required service adds pressure to already heavy schedules; students should volunteer freely, not for credit."
04:00
Word count: 0 words (Goal: 60+)
Chunk example (4 chunks ≈ 80 words, references Nina, concedes to Tom):

I firmly agree with Nina that community service should be required before graduation. [chunk 1 — stance + classmate] This is primarily because students acquire knowledge better when they apply it in real-world settings, which builds both competence and confidence. [chunk 2 — reason] For example, tutoring younger students helps future teachers gain experience while providing evidence that they can communicate complex ideas clearly. [chunk 3 — example] While Tom raises a fair concern about heavy schedules, universities can address issues of time by integrating service into existing courses rather than adding extra credits. [chunk 4 — concession + counter]

Checklist: 60+ words in 4 minutes? No mid-sentence editing (chunks only)? Stance clear in sentence 1? One classmate referenced? Concession clean ("While…, universities can…" — no "Although…but")? No known spelling traps?

Fix 2: Email Bullet-2-First Planning (8 min)

Write your strongest content in bullet 2 (the middle). Bullet 1 = context. Bullet 3 = offer/close. Bullet 2 = your most specific detail.

Write an Email (TOEFL format, 7 min): You are a second-year student who wants to join a research group next semester. Write an email to Professor Hayes. In your email, do the following:
1. Explain why you are interested in her machine-learning research group.
2. Describe relevant coursework or experience you have.
3. Offer to provide your transcript or meet during office hours.
Formal register. Target: 100–120 words (minimum 100).
07:00
Word count: 0 words (Goal: 100-120)
Model (~111 words, formal, uses "conduct research" + "draw conclusions"):

Dear Professor Hayes, I am writing to express my interest in joining your machine-learning research group next semester. I have followed your published work on natural-language processing and find the approach to evaluating model accuracy particularly relevant to my own academic goals.

Last semester I completed coursework in statistics and data analysis, where I learned to conduct research with Python and draw conclusions from datasets. I also contributed to a group project on sentiment analysis, which gave me practical experience with the methods your group uses.

I would be happy to provide my transcript or meet during office hours to discuss how I could contribute. Thank you for considering my request. Best regards, Valeria

Checklist: All 3 bullets present? Bullet 2 has specifics (course names, Python, datasets, project)? ≥100 words? Formal register? No known spelling traps? No "Although…but"?
