🎙️ Shadowing — Sentence Variety
Experienced academic writers vary their sentence structures to maintain reader engagement and demonstrate linguistic range. Simple sentences deliver direct claims with clarity. Compound sentences connect equal ideas using coordinating conjunctions. Complex sentences establish relationships of cause, condition, or concession through subordinate clauses. The most sophisticated responses combine all three types — simple for emphasis, compound for balance, complex for nuance. This variety signals to examiners that the writer possesses both grammatical control and the ability to organise ideas at multiple levels of complexity.

Targets: linguistic range · simple/compound/complex · grammatical control · multiple levels of complexity

🧩 The 4 Sentence Types

Aim for variety. Don't write all simple sentences (repetitive) or all complex (hard to follow). Mix them.

SIMPLE One independent clause. Clear, direct.

"Universities should extend library hours."

COMPOUND Two independent clauses joined by and/but/or/so.

"Students benefit from quiet spaces, and the library provides them."

COMPLEX Independent clause + dependent clause (because, although, when, if).

"Although some argue against extended hours, many students rely on them during finals."

COMPOUND-COMPLEX Two independent clauses + at least one dependent clause.

"When universities extend hours, students study more effectively, and exam results improve."

TOEFL scoring: "Displays consistent facility in the use of sentence structure" = variety matters. Repetitive sentence openings reduce the score.

🎯 Mini-Drill: Label the Types

Label each sentence as S (simple), Cm (compound), Cx (complex), or Cc (compound-complex):

1. "Technology has transformed education." [?]
2. "Students learn online, and they also attend campus lectures." [?]
3. "Because recorded lectures are available, students can review material at their own pace." [?]
4. "Although online learning offers flexibility, it lacks the social interaction that campus life provides." [?]
1. S — Simple (one independent clause)
2. Cm — Compound (two independent clauses joined by "and")
3. Cx — Complex (dependent clause "Because…" + independent clause)
4. Cx — Complex (dependent clause "Although…" + independent clause)
🔀 Range Check — Four Upgrades

Same idea, stronger structure. Upgrade each pair — decide the best version, then check.

1. "Cities need housing. They also need parks." → combine (balance)
2. "Exams are stressful. They are necessary." → combine (concession)
3. "Students can study abroad. This develops independence." → one sentence, gerund opening
4. "People say that internships help careers." → impersonal passive
1. Cities need housing, and they also need parks. Compound — coordinating conjunction joins equal ideas.
2. Although exams are stressful, they are necessary. Complex — subordinate clause adds nuance.
3. Studying abroad develops independence. Gerund as subject; also possible: Students go abroad to develop independence.
4. It is said that internships help careers. / Internships are said to help careers. Impersonal passive — formal claims without "people".

⚡ Proofread pass: scan your openings — if two sentences start the same way, upgrade one with a gerund, a subordinate clause, or the impersonal passive.

✍️ Sprint #1 — Academic Discussion (10 min)
Academic Discussion — TOEFL format: 10:00

Your professor is teaching a course on personal development. Write a post responding to the professor's question. In your post, you should: express and support your opinion; make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

Professor: "This week we are discussing study abroad. Does living in another country develop personal growth more than studying at home?"

Nina: "Living abroad teaches independence and adaptability — things no classroom can give you."
Tom: "I stayed home for university and still grew a lot: part-time jobs and clubs taught me the same lessons for much less money."

Focus today: Use all 4 sentence types (simple, compound, complex, compound-complex). Aim for variety — don't start every sentence the same way. Chunk-write. 120–130 words.

Model (~120 words, agrees with Nina, concedes to Tom, all 4 sentence types):

Although studying abroad is expensive, I agree with Nina that it develops personal growth more deeply than a normal semester at home. Living in a new country forces you to manage budgets, homes, and friendships in a second language, and every small task becomes a lesson in independence. My cousin, for example, returned from six months in Berlin far more confident: she now plans her own projects and speaks up in meetings without fear. However, Tom's concern about fairness is real, so universities should fund scholarships and short exchanges that keep the experience open to everyone. In short, growth depends less on distance than on challenge. Living abroad supplies both, but universities must make it affordable for every student.

Checklist: 120–130 words? Clear stance (agrees with Nina, concedes to Tom)? One classmate referenced? All 4 sentence types (S/Cm/Cx/Cc)? ≥2 transitions? Closing = synthesis, no new evidence? No "Although…but"?

Write an Email — TOEFL format: 07:00

You are a student in the science faculty. Write an email to the science department. In your email, do the following:

1. Explain that the lab equipment is outdated.
2. Describe how this affects your practical coursework.
3. Request new equipment or a maintenance schedule.

Target: 100–120 words (minimum 100). Formal register.

Focus today: Bullet-2-first. Use ≥2 sentence types. Target 100–120 words. Reserve last 60s for proofread pass.

Model (~108 words, all 3 tasks covered, each bullet = a mini T-S-C):

Dear Science Department,

I am writing about a problem that affects my practical coursework: several pieces of lab equipment in the main teaching lab are outdated and frequently break down.

As a result, my lab group often works in pairs on single machines, so we lose valuable practice time. Last week our microscope failed during an assessed practical, and three students could not complete the task.

A possible solution would be a maintenance schedule for the current equipment, supported by a small budget for replacements each semester.

I would be happy to collect student feedback about which machines fail most often. Thank you for considering this request.

Best regards,
Valeria

Checklist: All 3 tasks covered? Each bullet = a mini T-S-C paragraph? ≥100 words? Formal register? ≥2 transitions (e.g. As a result / A possible solution)? Proofread pass done?
