🎙️ Shadowing: Cognitive Load Theory
Cognitive load theory posits that human working memory has a strictly limited capacity, which can easily become overloaded if instructional material is poorly designed. Educational psychologists differentiate between intrinsic load—the inherent difficulty of the topic—and extraneous load, which stems from unnecessary or confusing presentation formats. By minimizing extraneous load through structured visual organization and clear signposting, educators can maximize germane load, enabling students to construct robust cognitive schemas. Understanding these cognitive constraints is fundamental to optimizing both computer-assisted learning and high-stakes test performance.

🎧 Lecture 1: Social Loafing in Group Dynamics

📝 Cornell Notes — symbols only (↑ ↓ → ≠ w/ w/o ! e.g.), no full sentences:

"Today we're exploring social loafing—the psychological phenomenon where individuals exert less effort when working collectively in a group than when working individually. First identified by Max Ringelmann in rope-pulling experiments, social loafing occurs because individual accountability diminishes as group size increases.

However, modern organizational psychologists emphasize that social loafing is not inevitable. When individual contributions are clearly monitored and evaluated—or when group members perceive the task as uniquely important—social loafing drops dramatically. Thus, the solution is not to eliminate group collaboration, but to structure tasks so that personal responsibility remains transparent."

Q1 (Gist): What is the main topic of the lecture?
(A) How to eliminate group work in educational settings.
(B) How individual effort decreases in group settings and how to prevent it.
(C) The history of Max Ringelmann's rope-pulling experiments.
(D) Why individual work is always superior to group work.
Q2 (Attitude): What is the professor's attitude toward group collaboration?
(A) Strongly critical — believes group work always fails.
(B) Constructive/Balanced — believes group work is valuable if individual accountability is maintained.
(C) Indifferent — has no particular opinion on group work.
(D) Enthusiastic — believes group work is always superior to individual work.
Q1: (B) — The lecture covers how social loafing reduces effort in groups and presents solutions (monitoring, task importance).
Q2: (B) — The professor says "the solution is not to eliminate group collaboration" but to structure tasks for personal responsibility — a balanced, constructive stance.

🎧 Lecture 2: Cognitive Load & Test Performance

📝 Cornell Notes — symbols only (↑ ↓ → ≠ w/ w/o ! e.g.), no full sentences:

"Extraneous cognitive load occurs when students must process poorly organized material. For example, if a diagram is separated from its explanation text on different pages, students waste working memory switching back and forth—this is called the split-attention effect.

Research by Sweller and colleagues demonstrated that integrating visual and verbal information directly—placing text labels on diagrams rather than in separate captions—significantly reduces extraneous load and improves learning outcomes. The key principle is redundancy: when the same information is presented in two separate formats that must be mentally integrated, working memory is wasted on integration rather than understanding."

Q1 (Detail): What causes the split-attention effect?
(A) Giving students too much information at once.
(B) Separating visual diagrams from their explanatory text.
(C) Using overly complex vocabulary in lecture slides.
(D) Presenting information only in written form.
Q2 (Inference): What would lower extraneous cognitive load in a lecture slide?
(A) Adding more text to explain each diagram.
(B) Placing text labels directly adjacent to visual elements.
(C) Removing all images from the presentation.
(D) Using smaller fonts to fit more content on each slide.
Q1: (B) — The text explicitly defines split-attention effect as occurring when "a diagram is separated from its explanation text on different pages."
Q2: (B) — The lecture states that "placing text labels on diagrams rather than in separate captions" reduces extraneous load. This directly supports B.
