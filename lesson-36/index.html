Shadowing — today's focus

An interview answer has four moves. Answer directly, give a reason, add one concrete detail, and stop cleanly. Do not restart the first sentence. Thirty to forty-five seconds is enough if every sentence adds something new.

Section 1 · Reading

Reading

Complete the Words

Official 2026 format: 70–100 words · 10 blanks · first sentence intact · second half of every second word removed · one dash per missing letter · exact spelling.

Students often believe that rereading a chapter is the best way to prepare for an exam. Research o memory sugg a diff method: tes yourself for you t retrieve infor instead o simply recog it. I one study, students who quizzed themselves remembered more after a week than students who read the same chapter four times. The effort of recall, even when it feels difficult, is what makes the knowledge stay. A short self-test at the end of each study session can therefore be more useful than another hour of reading.

Set A — Study habits

I usually study in the evening, after dinner. The main reason is that my school day is busy and my head is full of classes until four, so I need a break before I can concentrate. At around seven the apartment is quiet, and I can work for two hours without stopping. For example, last week I finished a whole history chapter between seven and nine, which would have taken me twice as long in the morning. So evenings work better for me because the environment is calmer.
45 s · no prep · no notes
Show a model answer (~110 words)👁️
One habit I would like to change is checking my phone while I study. I usually keep it on the desk, and every notification pulls me out of the task. The problem is not the phone itself; it is the few minutes it takes to get back into the material. I have tried putting it in another room, and my concentration improved immediately. I would like to make that a routine rather than an experiment, because even small interruptions add up over a week.
45 s · no prep · no notes
Show a model answer (~110 words)👁️
Last term I tried retrieval practice before a biology exam. Instead of rereading my notes, I closed the book and wrote everything I could remember, then checked the gaps. The first attempt was uncomfortable because I remembered less than I expected, but the gaps showed me exactly what to review. After three sessions I could explain the whole topic without notes, and I scored much higher than in the previous test. The method helped because it turned studying into a test of understanding rather than a feeling of familiarity.

Show a model answer (~110 words)👁️
I think it depends on the stage of the work, but if I have to choose, studying alone is more effective for me. When I study alone, I can move at my own speed and spend time on the parts I do not understand. In a group, the discussion is useful, but it can also become a social hour. For example, my group project meetings are productive, yet my best revision happens at my desk. A good compromise is to study alone first and then meet to explain ideas to each other.
