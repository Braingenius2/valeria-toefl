📋 TOEFL "Take an Interview" Speaking Format

The "Take an Interview" task simulates a short oral interview. You will answer 3–5 questions on a given topic with 45 seconds per response and zero prep time between questions.

  1. Listen to each question (played once only).
  2. Respond immediately in 45 seconds with a clear opinion + reasoning.
  3. Structure: Use OREO — Opinion, Reason, Example, Opinion restated.
Scoring Criteria (ETS): Delivery (fluency & clarity), Language Use (grammar & vocabulary), Topic Development (coherent structure, OREO).
🧱 OREO Structure for TOEFL Speaking
LetterTimeFunctionExample Phrase
O~5sOpinion — State your position clearly"I strongly believe that..." / "In my view..."
R~15sReason — Give your main rationale"The main reason is that..." / "This is because..."
E~15sExample — Support with a specific instance"For example,..." / "Consider the case of..."
O~10sOpinion Restated — Conclude with conviction"That's why I believe..." / "Ultimately, ..."

✍️ Academic Discussion — AI-Generated Content Regulation
👨‍🏫 Professor's Prompt

"Let's discuss the regulation of AI-generated content online. Some argue that governments must step in to require labeling of all AI-generated media to prevent deception and protect democratic processes. Others contend that regulation would be ineffective, hard to enforce, and could stifle innovation. What's your perspective? Should governments mandate transparency labels for AI-generated content?"

👩 Priya (classmate):

"I believe mandatory labeling is essential. Without regulation, people cannot distinguish real from fake, and that erodes trust in everything we see online. Just as food products list ingredients, AI-generated content should be clearly marked. Transparency is the only way to maintain accountability in the digital space."

👨 Leo (classmate):

"I disagree. Labeling requirements are impractical — who will enforce them? Bad actors will simply ignore the rules, while responsible creators bear the burden. Moreover, many AI tools are used for harmless creative purposes like art and entertainment. Heavy regulation could slow down innovation and punish legitimate users."

Model A — Concession + Counter (Balanced) · ~115 words

"While Leo raises a valid concern about the practical challenges of enforcement, I believe that mandating transparency labels for AI-generated content is a necessary first step toward maintaining public trust in digital media. Priya's comparison to food labelling is apt — regulation creates a norm even when compliance is imperfect. Nevertheless, the difficulty of enforcement does not justify inaction; it simply means we need a multi-layered approach that combines legal requirements with technological detection tools and media literacy education. In my view, establishing clear accountability frameworks for platforms that distribute undisclosed AI-generated content would incentivise responsible innovation while preserving the benefits of synthetic media for legitimate purposes."

Without such accountability measures, we risk a future where disinformation becomes indistinguishable from factual reporting. While I understand concerns about enforcement, historical precedent shows that regulation often drives innovation in compliance rather than stifling it."

Model C — Cautious Support (Leans Leo) · ~115 words

A more targeted approach could focus on high-risk domains such as political advertising and news media, where the potential for harm is greatest, while allowing flexibility for creative and educational uses of synthetic media. Ultimately, a combination of industry self-regulation, technological detection tools, and public education may prove more sustainable than broad government intervention."

concession: "While I appreciate... I share" references: Priya + Leo target vocab: transparency, detection, synthetic
