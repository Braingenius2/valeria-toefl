📋 TOEFL Academic Discussion Format

The Academic Discussion task replaced the Independent Essay in the 2026 TOEFL reform. You must:

  1. Read (1 min): A professor's prompt introducing a topic + two student responses.
  2. Write (10 min): A 100+ word contribution that agrees, disagrees, or partially agrees with the existing posts.
  3. Structure: State your position clearly → Add a new point/evidence → Acknowledge and build on or counter a classmate's point → Conclude.
Scoring Criteria (ETS): Content & Development (new perspective), Language Use (academic register), Coherence (logical flow).
📝 Three Response Templates

1. AGREE + ADD

"I strongly agree with [name]'s point about [topic]. In fact, research has shown that [new evidence]. Furthermore, [elaboration]. This suggests that [conclusion]."

2. DISAGREE + COUNTER

"While I see [name]'s perspective, I would argue that [counterpoint]. The reason is that [explanation]. For example, [specific example]. Therefore, [conclusion]."

3. PARTIALLY AGREE (Nuanced)

"I agree with the premise that [shared ground]; however, I think the issue is more nuanced because [complication]. On one hand, [acknowledge opposing view]. On the other hand, [your position]. Ultimately, [balanced conclusion]."

🔗 Concession & Counter-Argument Structures
FunctionStructure
Conceding a point"While it is true that...", "I acknowledge that...", "Admittedly,..."
Introducing your counter"However, this overlooks...", "Nevertheless,...", "That said,..."
Building on someone's idea"Building on [name]'s observation,...", "[Name] raises an important point about..., and I would add that..."
Introducing new evidence"A further consideration is...", "What is often overlooked is...", "An additional factor is..."

❌ "The question is why do people accept surveillance." → ✅ "The question is why people accept surveillance." (Indirect Question Word Order)

💬 Sample Academic Discussion Thread
👨‍🏫 Professor Chen:

"Let's discuss the impact of AI-powered surveillance in public spaces. Some argue that facial recognition technology and smart cameras make our cities safer by helping law enforcement identify suspects quickly. Others worry that this creates a surveillance state where citizens lose their privacy. What's your perspective? Do the security benefits justify the privacy costs?"

👩 Sophia (classmate):

"I think the benefits definitely outweigh the risks. In London, CCTV cameras have helped solve countless crimes, and facial recognition is just the next logical step. If you're not doing anything wrong, you have nothing to hide. Plus, AI can analyze footage much faster than humans can."

👨 Marcus (classmate):

"I disagree with Sophia. The 'nothing to hide' argument is dangerous — it assumes governments will always use surveillance responsibly, which history shows isn't true. Once surveillance infrastructure is built, it can easily be abused. We need strong legal protections before implementing these systems."

Tap to reveal answers. Use academic register vocabulary and concession frames.

1. Identify the template: Which response structure does Sophia use? Which does Marcus use?
Answer: Sophia = AGREE + ADD (adds the "nothing to hide" and speed arguments). Marcus = DISAGREE + COUNTER (counters the "nothing to hide" argument with the abuse of power concern).
2. Gap-fill — Complete: "__________ facial recognition can help solve crimes, it also raises serious privacy concerns."
Answer: "While it is true that facial recognition can help solve crimes, it also raises serious privacy concerns."
3. Gap-fill — Complete: "Sophia makes a valid point about efficiency; __________, efficiency without safeguards can be dangerous."
Answer: "...Sophia makes a valid point about efficiency; nevertheless, efficiency without safeguards can be dangerous."
4. Rewrite with academic register: "I think Sophia is wrong because cameras don't stop all crime and the government might use them for bad things."
Answer: "While I understand Sophia's perspective, I would argue that surveillance technology does not eliminate crime entirely. Moreover, there is a legitimate risk that such systems could be repurposed for objectives beyond public safety."
5. Choose your position and write an opening sentence (2-3 sentences) using one of the three templates.
Model Answer (PARTIALLY AGREE): "I agree with Marcus that privacy protections must precede surveillance expansion; however, I also see the practical benefits Sophia mentions. The key is not whether to use AI surveillance, but how to implement it with robust oversight mechanisms."
6. Error correction — Fix: "The professor explain why does surveillance matter in modern society."
Answer: "The professor explains why surveillance matters in modern society."
7. Structure identification — Label each rhetorical move: "Admittedly, facial recognition has helped solve crimes. However, this overlooks the fact that the technology has a significant racial bias problem. A 2019 MIT study found that commercial AI systems misidentified dark-skinned women up to 35% of the time. This suggests that until the technology improves, we cannot trust it as a primary law enforcement tool."
Answer: "Admittedly..." = Concession. "However..." = Counter-argument introduction. "A 2019 MIT study..." = New evidence. "This suggests that..." = Conclusion.
8. Write a full response (100+ words) using one of the three templates. Reference Sophia's and/or Marcus's posts.
Model Answer: "I largely agree with Marcus's concerns about surveillance infrastructure, but I'd add that the debate is not binary. On one hand, facial recognition technology has demonstrated measurable benefits — London's CCTV network has contributed to a significant reduction in street crime. On the other hand, as Marcus rightly points out, the potential for abuse is real. What is often overlooked is the possibility of intermediate solutions: AI surveillance with strict sunset clauses, independent oversight boards, and mandatory transparency reports. Ultimately, the question is not whether to use the technology, but under what conditions. A balanced approach — leveraging security benefits while enshrining privacy protections — seems the most prudent path forward."
