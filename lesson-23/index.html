🎙️ Shadowing — Transitions in Academic Writing
Effective academic writing flows logically from one idea to the next through the strategic use of transition words and phrases. These connectors serve as signposts that guide the reader through the writer's reasoning. Contrast transitions such as however and nevertheless signal a shift in perspective. Additive transitions like moreover and furthermore build upon previous points. Causal transitions including consequently and therefore establish logical chains. Mastering these devices transforms disconnected sentences into a coherent, persuasive argument that meets the expectations of formal academic discourse.

Targets: strategic use · signposts · contrast transitions · additive transitions · causal transitions · coherent argument

🔧 The Transition Toolkit

Use transitions to connect ideas across sentences. Each type serves a different logical function. Vary them — don't repeat the same one in every paragraph.

Contrast: however, nevertheless, nonetheless, on the other hand, conversely, although
Additive: moreover, furthermore, in addition, additionally, also
Causal: consequently, therefore, as a result, thus, hence, accordingly
Example: for instance, for example, to illustrate, specifically, such as
Conclusion: in conclusion, to summarise, overall, ultimately, in summary

⚠️ TOEFL trap: Don't use "Although… but" together. Pick one. Also, avoid tautological closers — end with a concrete point, not a generic "In conclusion."

✍️ Comma rule: sentence-initial transition + comma (However, the results differ.) · mid-sentence transition wrapped in commas (The results, however, differ.) · never join two sentences with only a comma — use a full stop or semicolon. Sequencing extras: subsequently, previously, meanwhile.

🎯 Mini-Drill: Insert Transitions

Read the paragraph. Identify where transitions are missing and add them:

"University education is expensive. [?] many students benefit from part-time work to offset costs. Working students gain practical experience. [?] they may struggle to balance responsibilities. Employers value graduates with work experience. [?] students who work during their studies often have stronger CVs."
"University education is expensive. Moreover, many students benefit from part-time work to offset costs. Working students gain practical experience. However, they may struggle to balance responsibilities. Employers value graduates with work experience. Consequently, students who work during their studies often have stronger CVs."
✍️ Sprint #1 — Academic Discussion (10 min)
Academic Discussion — TOEFL format: 10:00

Your professor is teaching a course on education technology. Write a post responding to the professor's question. In your post, you should: express and support your opinion; make a contribution to the discussion in your own words.

Professor: "This week we are discussing online learning. Are online classes just as effective as traditional in-person classes, or is face-to-face instruction essential for meaningful learning?"

Nina: "Online classes are flexible and let students learn at their own pace, so more people can access education."
Tom: "Face-to-face discussion builds real relationships and forces active participation that a screen cannot replace."

Focus today: Use ≥3 different transitions (one contrast, one additive, one causal). Chunk-write. Target 120–130 words. Do NOT stop to edit mid-sentence.

Model (~128 words, agrees with Tom): transitions colour-coded — contrast, additive, causal, example.

While I understand Nina's point that online classes are flexible and widen access, I believe Tom is right that face-to-face instruction is essential for meaningful learning. This is primarily because live discussion forces students to think on their feet and respond to counterarguments immediately, which builds deeper understanding. For example, a seminar in which students debate a case study aloud develops critical thinking far more effectively than watching recorded lectures alone. Moreover, campus interaction creates trust, and trust encourages quieter students to contribute. Consequently, universities should keep face-to-face seminars at the core of their programmes. Online materials can certainly complement those seminars, but they cannot replace the energy and accountability of a real classroom. Therefore, a blended model is valuable, but the live seminar must remain the centrepiece.

Model (~111 words, all 3 tasks covered, specific details):

Dear Library Director,

I am writing to ask whether the library could trial extended evening hours during exam periods. In my view, later closing times would greatly benefit students.

At present, the library closes at 8 p.m., so students who work or attend late seminars have almost no quiet place to study during the week. Consequently, many of us end up writing assignments at home, where distractions reduce the quality of our work.

I understand that extending hours increases staffing costs. Therefore, I would like to suggest a two-month trial on weekdays until 11 p.m., after which the university could evaluate attendance.

Thank you for considering this request.

Best regards,
Valeria

Checklist: All 3 tasks covered? Task 2 has specifics (8 p.m. closing, work/late seminars, home distractions)? ≥100 words? ≥2 transitions with correct comma use? Formal register? No known spelling traps?
